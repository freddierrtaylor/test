## OVERVIEW

Create **AFTERSHIFT**, an automotive discovery and collection game set in a fictionalised Bahrain and the wider Gulf.

The player starts with an inexpensive car, a modest workshop and very little money. They explore a world inspired by Bahrain, uncover rare vehicles, meet local collectors, discover hidden garages, buy and restore cars, and work their way into the region's exclusive motorsport scene.

The game must feel distinctly Middle Eastern. It should not be a generic racing game with a desert texture added.

## SETTING AND WORLD: BAHRAIN AND THE GULF

The entire game must take place in a visually rich world inspired by Bahrain and the wider Gulf region. Do not use Japanese settings, Japanese mountain passes, Tokyo street culture or Japanese-themed environments.

Create a fictionalised, explorable automotive region inspired by Bahrain's geography, architecture, climate, lifestyle and motorsport heritage.

The environment should feel authentic to the Gulf. Use regional references as the foundation for the world rather than applying generic desert scenery to a conventional racing game.

### 1. A motorsport complex inspired by Bahrain International Circuit

Create a major racing venue inspired by Bahrain International Circuit.

Include a race circuit, pit garages, paddock, grandstands, track-day events, time trials, drag racing facilities where appropriate, night racing and advanced motorsport challenges.

Use the desert surroundings, modern circuit architecture and dramatic floodlighting to create memorable racing environments.

Players start with amateur time trials and progress towards prestigious racing events, track days and endurance challenges.

### 2. A Manama-inspired urban district

Build a modern Gulf city featuring contemporary skyscrapers, waterfront roads, hotels, shopping districts, underground parking, premium car showrooms and illuminated streets.

Create a convincing atmosphere at different times of day, especially during warm sunset light and late-night cruising.

Add cafés, automotive workshops, car parks and social gathering locations where players encounter other enthusiasts.

### 3. Desert highways and remote locations

Build long, open desert highways, sweeping interchanges, sand-covered landscapes, industrial roads and remote compounds.

Place hidden garages, abandoned project cars, specialist parts, rare vehicle discoveries and secret challenges throughout these areas. Exploration should reveal cars and secrets rather than simply provide empty scenery.

Driving through the desert should feel different from driving in the city or on a race circuit. Implement suitable handling, road surfaces, dust effects and environmental audio.

### 4. Gulf automotive culture

Represent the region's varied car culture through vehicle collections, social gatherings, modification shops, luxury vehicles, performance cars, SUVs, drag racing, drifting and circuit motorsport.

Include ordinary daily drivers alongside expensive supercars and rare collector vehicles. The player should begin with modest means and gradually earn access to prestigious cars and exclusive automotive communities. Build a strong sense of local community and status.

Use culturally appropriate environmental details, Arabic and English signage where suitable, regional architecture, local road infrastructure, realistic lighting and appropriate clothing for NPCs.

Avoid stereotypical or superficial representations of Gulf life. Make the world feel lived in and credible.

### 5. Automotive discovery and collecting

Discovery is built around rare vehicles, abandoned builds, limited-production cars, forgotten racing machines and unique automotive parts.

Vehicles should appear in context. An old project car might sit inside a remote workshop, a rare collector car might appear at an exclusive gathering, and a motorsport vehicle might require completing a circuit challenge.

Use rarity, clues, collector relationships, restoration requirements and exploration to make every discovery meaningful.

### 6. Regional identity and visual quality

The visual identity should combine warm desert colours, pale sand, modern concrete and glass architecture, coastal blues, dramatic sunset skies and the contrast of bright daylight with illuminated night environments.

Prioritise detailed vehicle paintwork, convincing road surfaces, atmospheric lighting, high-quality reflections and carefully composed environments.

The final result should feel like a premium automotive adventure set in Bahrain, with enough fictionalised locations and discoveries to support an original game world.

Use original assets or properly licensed references. Treat real locations as visual inspiration, and do not claim the fictional map is an exact representation of Bahrain.

## GAMEPLAY SYSTEMS

### Collecting and the car register

- Every vehicle the player sees, drives, owns or hears about goes into a **car register**, the game's collection log.
- Each entry records where and when it was found, its condition, its history, who owned it before and how complete the player's knowledge of it is.
- Registers are grouped into sets, such as Gulf daily drivers, 4x4s and desert vehicles, drag builds, circuit cars, classic imports and limited-production supercars. Completing a set unlocks rewards, contacts and events.

### Rarity

- Use clear rarity tiers: **Common, Uncommon, Rare, Legendary and One-off**.
- Rarity comes from the car's history and condition as well as its model. A common saloon with a documented local racing past can be worth more than a newer supercar.
- The rarest cars are never sold openly. They come through clues, trust and challenges.

### Clues and leads

- Leads come from conversations at cafés and car meets, old photographs, classified adverts, workshop gossip, paperwork found inside other cars and collectors who start to trust the player.
- Each lead narrows a search area (a district, an industrial estate, a stretch of desert highway) without placing a marker on the exact spot. The player has to look.

### Buying, restoring and selling

- Cars are found in varying condition: running, tired, non-running, stripped or barely a shell.
- Restoration is a hands-on garage loop. The player diagnoses faults, sources parts, chooses between original and modified parts, and repairs the bodywork, interior, engine, drivetrain and paint.
- Sand, heat and salt air affect condition. Rubber perishes, interiors fade and coastal cars rust.
- Originality and documentation matter to collectors. Performance matters at the track and the drag strip. The player decides which to pursue for each car.
- Use a fictional currency modelled on the Bahraini dinar for prices.

### Progression

- Start with one cheap, ordinary car, a small rented workshop unit and almost no money.
- Earn money and reputation through time trials, track days, drag nights, car-meet challenges, deliveries, restoration jobs and selling cars.
- Reputation is tracked separately with different communities: the circuit, the drag scene, the drift scene, the off-road and desert crowd, classic collectors and the high-end supercar circle. Each opens its own events, contacts and invitations.
- Move up from amateur time trials to club racing, invitational events, night races and endurance challenges at the circuit.
- Upgrade the workshop from a single bay to a full facility with a paint booth, a dyno, specialist tools and staff.

### The garage

- The garage is the player's home base and a place to show off the collection.
- Players can walk around their cars, inspect them in detail and photograph them.
- The garage layout, lighting and display areas can be customised, and its standing affects which collectors will visit and trade.

### Social world and collectors

- Give collectors, mechanics, racers and shop owners their own personalities, collections, histories and preferences.
- Relationships are built through trades, favours, restorations and results on track, not just through money.
- Gatherings such as weekend car meets, late-night café meets and invitation-only collector events change through the week and with the time of day.

### Driving

- Handling should vary clearly between surfaces and places: grippy circuit asphalt, dusty city streets, sand-blown highways and loose desert tracks.
- Cars should feel distinct according to their weight, drivetrain, power and condition. A neglected car should feel worse than a restored one.
- Support a full day–night cycle, with heat haze, dust and night lighting as real parts of the driving experience.
